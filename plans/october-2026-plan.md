## Style rules (every video)

- Information only. No shop name, no logo end card with the shop name, no phone number, no call to action.
- Voice: calm and a little slow so the words land. Use the existing narrator voice at a slower pace (about 0.9x speed if the voice tool allows it, or add short pauses between sentences).
- Vertical 9:16, 1080x1920. Full videos 25 to 40 seconds (slow pace allowed up to 40 seconds). Cutdowns about 12 seconds.
- Use the on-screen text exactly as written below as the main title card.
- Use the voiceover scripts below word for word. Only shorten if a read runs over 40 seconds, and keep every technical fact.
- Any AI-generated visual is an educational illustration, not footage of a real repair.

## Credit-saving production rules

1. Reuse existing broll/ footage first. Match the scene description as closely as the library allows.
2. Paid AI video is capped at 3 short clips per week (8 seconds, veo_3_1_fast, 9:16, no audio, no text). Use them only for scenes the library cannot cover. Never generate a full-length AI video.
3. Make one master video per topic, then make the 12-second cutdown from that same master (first fact + on-screen text). Cutdowns cost no new generation.
4. One master file goes to both TikTok and YouTube. Never build separate platform versions.
5. Regenerate only for real failure: wrong part shown, unreadable text, wrong audio, obvious artifact. Do not regenerate for taste.
6. Keep a topic ledger (topic, hook, date, variant, views) in the repo so winners can be remixed later.

## Topic of the Day worksheet

**1. P0300 misfire**
Scene: Shop bay, engine on a lift, technician listening near the valve cover, then a spark plug and ignition coil on the bench.
On-screen text: P0300 — Random misfire
Voiceover: "P0300 means the engine is misfiring on more than one cylinder. You may feel a shake, a loss of power, or see the check engine light flashing. Common causes are worn spark plugs, a bad coil, a vacuum leak, or low fuel pressure. A flashing light means stop driving. A misfire can overheat and damage the catalytic converter. The right first step is finding which cylinder is actually missing, not replacing parts at random."

**2. P0171 too lean**
Scene: Close-up of a cracked intake hose, then a scan tool showing fuel trims.
On-screen text: P0171 — Too lean, Bank 1
Voiceover: "P0171 means Bank 1 is getting too much air or not enough fuel. The engine is running lean. Usual causes are a vacuum leak, a dirty mass airflow sensor, or a weak fuel pump. You might notice a rough idle or worse mileage. A smoke test for leaks and a fuel-pressure check usually narrow it down before anyone condemns a sensor."

**3. P0420 catalyst**
Scene: Undercar shot of a catalytic converter, then upstream and downstream oxygen sensors.
On-screen text: P0420 — Catalyst efficiency low
Voiceover: "P0420 means the catalytic converter is not cleaning the exhaust the way it should. It is not always a bad converter. A misfire, an exhaust leak, or a lazy oxygen sensor can set the same code. Replacing the converter first often wastes money. Sensors and any misfire should be confirmed before a cat is recommended."

**4. P0442 and P0455 EVAP leak**
Scene: Hand tightening a gas cap, then light smoke at an EVAP hose.
On-screen text: P0442 small leak / P0455 large leak
Voiceover: "P0442 is a small evaporative leak. P0455 is a large one. The most common cause is a loose or bad gas cap. If the cap is fine, it is often a cracked hose, a purge valve, or the charcoal canister. These codes usually do not change how the car drives, but they will fail a smog check. Tighten the cap, clear the code, and if it comes back, the leak has to be found."

**5. P0128 thermostat**
Scene: Temperature gauge staying low, then a thermostat next to a coolant hose.
On-screen text: P0128 — Engine not warming up
Voiceover: "P0128 means the engine is taking too long to reach normal temperature. The usual cause is a thermostat stuck open. The heater may feel weak and fuel economy can drop. This is not an overheat code. It is the opposite. Coolant level and actual temperature should be confirmed before the thermostat is replaced."

**6. P0401 EGR**
Scene: Carbon-clogged EGR passage, then a clean port.
On-screen text: P0401 — EGR flow too low
Voiceover: "P0401 means exhaust gas is not flowing back into the engine the way it should. On most cars this is carbon buildup in the EGR valve or the passages, not always a failed part. You may feel a ping or just see the check engine light. Cleaning the ports often fixes it. Flow should be tested before a new valve is sold."

**7. Car clicks, will not start**
Scene: Key turn, single click, battery terminals with white corrosion, then a jump pack.
On-screen text: Click, no start
Voiceover: "If the car only clicks when you turn the key, the battery is usually dead or the connection is bad. Corrosion on the terminals can act just like a bad battery. A weak battery can also make a good starter sound failed. Load-test the battery and check the cables before a starter is replaced."

**8. Cranks but will not start**
Scene: Engine cranking with no fire, then a fuel-pressure gauge and a spark plug.
On-screen text: Cranks, no start
Voiceover: "If it cranks but never fires, the battery is doing its job. The engine is missing fuel, spark, or both. Common causes are a failed fuel pump, a bad crank sensor, or no spark from the coils. Do not keep cranking. That only drains the battery. Fuel pressure and spark are the first two checks, so you are not guessing."

**9. Squeaking brakes**
Scene: Brake pad wear tab just touching the rotor, pad still has material.
On-screen text: Squeak = wear warning
Voiceover: "A light squeak when you brake is usually the wear indicator on the pad. It is telling you the pads are getting thin, not that the rotor is already ruined. Catch it now and it is often pads only. Wait, and the next noise is grinding. Pads should be measured before a full brake job is assumed."

**10. Grinding brakes**
Scene: Metal-on-metal rotor with a deep groove. Serious, not scary.
On-screen text: Grind = pads are gone
Voiceover: "A grinding noise when you brake means the pad material is gone and metal is cutting the rotor. That is no longer a pad-only job. Rotors are usually damaged, and stopping distance gets worse. Do not keep driving on it. Both sides should be inspected, because one side often wears faster."

**11. Steering wheel shakes**
Scene: Highway wheel shake, then a pulse only while braking. Split text: speed vs braking.
On-screen text: Shake at speed / shake when braking
Voiceover: "If the wheel shakes at highway speed, it is usually a tire out of balance or an alignment issue. If it shakes only when you hit the brakes, that is almost always a rotor with uneven thickness. Two different problems, two different fixes. When it happens matters more than the shake itself."

**12. Fluid puddles**
Scene: Three small puddles on concrete: dark, green-orange, and red.
On-screen text: Color tells you the system
Voiceover: "Dark brown or black under the engine is usually oil. Bright green, pink, or orange is coolant, and that can lead to overheating. Reddish fluid is often transmission fluid or power steering fluid. Do not just top it off and ignore it. A small leak becomes a bigger repair. The color points to the system. The drip point shows the source."

**13. Shakes at a red light**
Scene: Car idling with a slight shake in the mirror, then an air filter and a spark plug.
On-screen text: Rough idle
Voiceover: "If the car shakes while you are stopped, the engine is idling rough. A dirty air filter, worn spark plugs, a dirty mass airflow sensor, or a vacuum leak are the common causes. It is not always a major repair. A scan and a check of the basics come before parts are replaced."

**14. AC blowing warm** (AC Problem of the Week, Episode 1)
Scene: Dash set to cold, warm air from the vents, then AC gauges.
On-screen text: Warm air is not always low refrigerant
Voiceover: "AC blowing warm is a huge summer search, but a recharge kit is not always the fix. If the refrigerant is low, it leaked out. A bad compressor, a failed clutch, or a clogged orifice tube can do the same thing. Adding refrigerant to a leak only brings the problem back. Pressures should be checked before the system is charged."

## Problem of the Week 1: AC — what causes AC problems

Same calm, slow style, information only. The build writes and fact-checks scripts 2-7 (70-85 words each) from these outlines.

| Ep | On-screen text | Outline |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Warm air is not always low refrigerant | Topic 14 script above, word for word |
| 2 | Where AC leaks hide | Low refrigerant means a leak. Common spots: condenser behind the grille, hose crimps and O-rings, service port valves, compressor seal. Found with UV dye or an electronic leak detector. |
| 3 | Compressor clutch not engaging | Clutch should click on when AC is turned on. If it does not: blown fuse or relay, low-pressure switch blocking it because charge is low, worn clutch air gap. Check power and pressure before condemning the compressor. |
| 4 | Cold on the highway, warm at idle | Points to airflow over the condenser: failed cooling fan, debris-blocked condenser fins. Moving air at speed hides the problem. Fan operation is checked first. |
| 5 | Cold on one side only | Often a blend door actuator, sometimes a clicking noise behind the dash. Can also be a dual-zone control issue. Not a refrigerant problem. |
| 6 | AC cold but weak airflow | Clogged cabin air filter, failing blower motor or resistor, blocked evaporator. Cabin filter is checked first and is often a simple replacement. |
| 7 | Why recharge cans can backfire | Overcharging raises pressures and can damage the compressor. Some cans include sealer that can clog equipment. The correct fix: find the leak, repair it, vacuum the system, charge by weight to the spec on the underhood label. |

## Problem of the Week 2: Overheating (outlines; build researches and writes)

4. Cooling fan not turning on.
5. Water pump failure signs.

## Safety and accuracy guardrails (unchanged)

- Flashing check engine light or severe shaking: stop safely and arrange help.
- No DIY high-pressure fuel work, no flammable leak sprays, never open a hot cooling system.
- No invented prices, findings, or guarantees. Exact maintenance intervals require the exact vehicle schedule.
- AC: refrigerant handling requires certified recovery equipment; do not suggest venting refrigerant.
